How certain is that number?

An estimate has edges.

The model’s 95% confidence interval is 2.8–5.2%. It does not capture every uncertainty about the assumptions or about who was innocent.

Off death row.
Still not free.

A reduced sentence can end the threat of execution without clearing someone’s name. The researchers found that exoneration became much less likely after that threat ended.

They modeled what might be uncovered if everyone remained under the intense scrutiny associated with a death sentence. That is how they reached 4.1%—which they considered a conservative estimate of wrongful convictions.

Escaping execution can still mean
spending your life in prison.

The study, the assumptions, the numbers

The study followed 7,482 defendants. Its Kaplan–Meier model estimated exoneration under continued execution threat at 21.4 years after conviction. It accounts for different observation times and departures from death row.

Exoneration is an imperfect proxy for innocence. Departures may be related to innocence, too. The authors examined those assumptions in sensitivity analyses. Their “at least” interpretation is not a statistical lower bound. This result does not describe today’s death row or all criminal convictions.

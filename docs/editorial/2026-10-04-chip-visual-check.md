# Chip visual reader check

Prepared October 4, 2026. **Not run. No independent responses collected.**

The creator authorized a complete chip entry after the text walkthrough. That puts one visual treatment ahead of the independent-text-screening gate originally proposed. The current opening position is an experiment, not a launch decision. Existing creator responses remain in the [second reader check](2026-10-04-second-reader-check.md).

## First session

Recruit three to five people who have not seen the prototype or been told the chip discovery. This is a small qualitative check, not a representative survey. Let people use their own device where possible. Do not explain the fact or demonstrate the controls first.

Open the page at `index.html#top`. Say: “Explore this as you would a site someone sent you. You can leave whenever you like.” Audio is optional; record whether each person actually listens. Do not pressure them to turn it on.

Watch the first minute without coaching. Record the first action, whether they find the reveal, whether they try one or both sounds, where they hesitate, and whether they voluntarily continue into copper. A skipped entry is useful evidence. Do not interpret a click or a pause as delight.

After exploration, ask these separately:

1. What was that first discovery saying, in your own words?
2. Had you encountered the underlying idea before? Where?
3. What, if anything, surprised you?
4. Was anything confusing or awkward to use?
5. Is there someone you would tell? What would you actually say to them?
6. What do you remember from the page? Would you want another entry?

Only then ask about presentation: “What did you enjoy or dislike about how it looked and moved?” Keep those answers separate from novelty and comprehension. For audio users, ask what they thought the comparison demonstrated. If they think it proves freshness, reproduces the study, or shows that 75% of everyone is fooled, record that misunderstanding before clarifying.

## Record per reader

- Device, approximate viewport, sound used / sound declined / sound unavailable.
- Prior exposure to the site or topic.
- Observed first action, reveal, sound playback, reset, source disclosure, continuation.
- Exact explanations, surprise, retelling, and design comments.
- Any assistance given and when.
- Separate observed behavior, self-report, and our interpretation.

Do not fill blank responses with guesses. No recording or sharing of participant details is required for this check.

## Decision

Keep developing this opener if multiple fresh readers understand the perceptual mechanism without help, find it new, and offer a specific retelling. Treat voluntary continuation and visual enjoyment as additional signals. Fix confusing controls or wording before adding more animation. If attention stays on the chip image but readers cannot explain or care about the discovery, revise the entry rather than counting visual approval as success.

Compare silent and audio responses descriptively; this small, self-selected sample cannot establish that audio caused engagement. Leave room for a different opener if the evidence favors one.

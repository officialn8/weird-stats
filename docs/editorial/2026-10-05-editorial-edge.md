# Editorial direction after the copper study

Nate's October 5 feedback: the looser copper composition is better, but the facts themselves also feel rigid and "a little too lukewarm." He wants an "unabashedly honest" website. This feedback changes the selection brief; it does not approve new claims or authorize release.

## Working interpretation

Choose discoveries that can change how someone sees the world, including subjects with consequences. Power, money, work, institutions, incentives, and everyday systems belong in the research mix. An uncomfortable finding is eligible on its merits. The direction is not limited to politics, and the collection can still be funny or delightful.

The current collection leans toward charming curiosities. Preserve good light entries as variety, while testing a stronger editorial center: relationships that make a reader reconsider something, rather than simply remember an unusual number. This is an editorial judgment, not measured audience feedback.

## Selection and writing

- Look for a clear contradiction, hidden cost, unequal burden, unexpected benefit, or consequence. Identify who is affected and why the relationship matters.
- Let the evidence determine the conclusion, including findings that challenge the editor's own expectations. Do not choose a desired villain or emotional reaction before researching.
- State supported findings directly. Keep essential scope and uncertainty close to the claim; use source disclosures for additional detail. Avoid both euphemism and conclusions stronger than the evidence.
- An entry can be playful, disturbing, hopeful, or infuriating. Do not require a cheerful finish or provocative language.
- Judge the discovery after restoring its full context. If the tension disappears, the headline has not earned it.

## Research directions to discuss

These are questions to investigate, not established findings or approved assignments:

- When a company reports record profits, what happened to prices, worker pay, and output over the same period?
- What does a city spend settling misconduct claims, and who actually pays?
- How does changing the denominator change a familiar claim about representation, taxes, or public spending?

The next useful editorial experiment is a small, varied shortlist of sourced discoveries with greater consequence, shown in plain language before substantial visual work. Keep all new material private until reviewed. Existing publication and evidence rules still apply.

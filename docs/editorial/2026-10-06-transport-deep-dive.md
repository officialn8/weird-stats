# First Deep Dive: One person. An entire car.

Requested by Nate on October 6, 2026, inspired by Bill Nye’s Human Transportation episode. Nate later emphasized his immense passion for the topic and invited bold, creative execution. This is an explicit content request, not an automated daily selection or release authorization.

## Editorial approach

Keep 60 travelers fixed while changing the number of vehicles. Follow with separate chapters on lane capacity, fuel conversion, kinetic energy, mass and speed. End with the freedom to leave a car at home, while acknowledging disability-related, rural, work and other needs. No claim that every transit trip beats every car trip, and no blame assigned to travelers without useful alternatives.

## Mass and speed refinement — October 6

Nate requested a clearer treatment of heavy vehicles at speed. “Heavy metal. Fragile people.” uses the requested American presentation: illustrative vehicle weight from 2,000–8,000 lb in 500 lb steps and speed from 10–60 mph. The default 4,000 lb at 30 mph yields 120,345 foot-pounds and an equivalent ideal fall of 30.1 ft. The current fixed height scale spans 0–140 ft; the energy bar has been removed. Calculations use 1 mph = 0.44704 m/s, energy = ½mv², height = v²/(2g) and fall duration = v/g, with g = 9.81 m/s². Mass changes energy but not equivalent height; doubling speed quadruples energy and height. Calculations convert lb × 0.45359237 to kg, joules ÷ 1.3558179483314004 to foot-pounds, and meters ÷ 0.3048 to feet. Inside the calculation disclosure, energy displays to whole foot-pounds with commas and height to one decimal; the main height reading rounds to whole feet. At 4,000 lb, 20 mph gives 53,487 foot-pounds and 13.4 ft; 40 mph gives 213,947 foot-pounds and 53.5 ft. The energy ratio is exactly four before rounding. CSV scenarios cover 2,000/4,000/8,000 lb at 20/30/40/60 mph; the allocation table remains separate.

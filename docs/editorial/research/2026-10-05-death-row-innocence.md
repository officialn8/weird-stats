# Death-sentence innocence: evidence check

Checked October 5, 2026. Historical finding; not an estimate of today's death row.

Gross, O’Brien, Hu and Kennedy (2014), PNAS 111(20), 7230–7235. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1306417111.

## Extraction

| Item | Verified value / scope | Locator in mirror |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Population | 7,482 US defendants sentenced to death, 1973–2004 | p. 2, Data |
| Estimate | 4.1%; 95% confidence interval 2.8–5.2% | p. 3, Analysis |
| Method | Kaplan–Meier exoneration estimate at 21.4 years under continued execution threat | p. 3, Analysis; Figure 2 |
| Interpretation | Authors consider it a conservative proxy for wrongful convictions | pp. 4–5 |

Exoneration is an imperfect proxy for innocence. Censoring may be informative; the paper tests alternative assumptions. Its “at least” interpretation is not a confidence bound. The result cannot measure innocent executions or all criminal convictions. Resentencing can end execution risk while leaving a conviction intact; diminished scrutiny helps explain why innocence can remain undiscovered.

## Design constraints

Lead with the finding and its historical scope. Keep “estimated” attached to the number. Explain the model without implying that anyone has identified a particular innocent person in every group. No invented names, portraits, individual case stories, executions, or audience responses. An original visual may illustrate a proportion; it must not resemble a count of known cases. No source figures or research dataset files are incorporated.

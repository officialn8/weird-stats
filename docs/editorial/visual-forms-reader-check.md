# Visual forms reader check — round 1

Prepared October 4, 2026. **Not run. No independent responses collected.** This is the U4 protocol for the [product roadmap](../plans/2026-10-04-2200-feat-discovery-product-roadmap-plan.md). Preparing this document does not pass the reader gate.

## Before inviting anyone

The creator recruits three to five people who have not seen these discoveries, the prototype, or its answers. Use a separate group from both the chip visual check and the plain-text check. Do not send this moderator document to participants. Keep previews local; remote access requires a separately authorized way to share the review build.

First finish and inspect the paired-comparison study and its individual discovery page. Freeze the exact source revision and record it below. Do not mix changed prompts or designs within a round. Run the pending chip version 2 check separately on its exact local proposal preview with a different cohort; no member of this cohort may have seen the Senate draft through that check.

Use the Senate discovery page as the arrival experience. Test copper's penny pile and the painting's physical-scale sequence in the same session, recording each separately. These are prototypes for three different forms; success on one does not approve another. Alternate copper/painting order across participants after the arrival task. Every participant must be free to stop; showing a later scene at the moderator's request is prompted exposure, not voluntary continuation.

## Session

Say: “Explore this as you would a site someone sent you. You can stop whenever you like.” Do not explain the relationship or operate a control for the reader. Guessing and audio are optional.

Observe the first minute. Record first action, optional guess, reveal, hesitation, and whether the reader chooses to continue into the collection. Record whether a share control is found and what the reader expects it to do; do not require them to send a message.

After exploration, ask separately for each scene encountered:

1. “What was that discovery saying, in your own words?” Record the first explanation before helping.
2. “Had you encountered that underlying idea before? Where?” Familiarity means the relationship, not remembering its exact number.
3. “What, if anything, changed what you expected?”
4. “Who, if anyone, would you tell? What would you actually say?” Do not supply an example.
5. “What did you enjoy or dislike about how it looked and moved?”
6. “Was anything confusing or awkward to use?”

Record observed continuation separately from a later statement that they would like more. Keep design enjoyment, novelty, comprehension, and retelling as separate measures. Do not infer delight from dwell time, a reveal click, or politeness.

For the Senate scene, listen for both population and seats, the direction of the comparison, and the fact that these are 2020 residents rather than current voters. For copper, listen for copper mass rather than money. For painting, listen for a photograph's pixel scale rather than pixels physically in the painting. Mark any explanation supplied after assistance separately.

## Record without identifying people

Copy the [blank round sheet](reader-results/round-1-template.md) for actual observations. Use aliases, device/viewport, source revision, exposure order, and exact short quotations. Do not collect names, contact details, or recordings. Leave unknowns unknown. Preserve earlier creator responses in their original documents.

## Decision per form

Use three to five unexposed readers. A form passes the exploratory comprehension gate only when at least three report the underlying relationship as unfamiliar and all but one of those unfamiliar readers explain it without coaching. A majority of all readers must voluntarily continue to another discovery. Report familiar readers' explanations separately. Fewer than three unfamiliar readers makes the round inconclusive, not a pass.

Apply the gate independently to paired comparison, copper's Unit Pile prototype, and painting's Scale Drop prototype. A skipped prototype has missing evidence. Do not turn a prompted visit into continuation credit. A failure calls for revision and a fresh round. Passing allows further form development; it does not establish retention, actual sharing, or broad audience demand.

U5 stays gated until the relevant prototype passes. Seven real research runs and seven dated Spread rehearsals are separate later gates; this reader check cannot substitute for either.
